Mercury

Mercury, the smallest planet in our Solar System, is a rocky world of extremes. Named after the swift messenger of the Roman gods, it orbits the Sun faster than any other planet.

  • Size: 4,879 km diameter
  • Age: 4.503 billion years
  • Rotational Speed: 10.83 km/h
  • Axis Tilt: 2.04°
  • Orbit Period: 88 Earth days
  • Day Length: 1,408 hours (58.65 Earth days)
  • Temperature: -180°C to 430°C

Fun Fact: Mercury's surface resembles our Moon with numerous impact craters. Despite being the closest planet to the Sun, some of its craters contain water ice in permanent shadow.

Venus

Venus, often called Earth's twin due to its similar size and mass, is a world of extreme heat and pressure. Named after the Roman goddess of love and beauty, it's the hottest planet in our Solar System.

  • Size: 12,104 km diameter
  • Age: 4.503 billion years
  • Rotational Speed: 6.52 km/h (retrograde)
  • Axis Tilt: 177.3°
  • Orbit Period: 225 Earth days
  • Day Length: 2,802 hours (116.75 Earth days)
  • Temperature: 462°C (average)

Fun Fact: Venus rotates backwards compared to most planets, with the sun rising in the west and setting in the east.

Earth

Earth, our home planet, is the only known world to support life. It's a water world, with two-thirds of the planet covered by ocean. Earth's atmosphere is rich in nitrogen and oxygen.

  • Size: 12,742 km diameter
  • Age: 4.543 billion years
  • Rotational Speed: 1,674.4 km/h
  • Axis Tilt: 23.5°
  • Orbit Period: 365.25 days
  • Day Length: 24 hours
  • Temperature: -88°C to 58°C

Fun Fact: Earth is the only planet not named after a god. The name comes from the Old English word "ertha" meaning ground or land.

Mars

Mars, often called the Red Planet, has long fascinated humans with its rusty-red appearance and potential for harboring life. It's home to the largest volcano and canyon in the Solar System.

  • Size: 6,779 km diameter
  • Age: 4.603 billion years
  • Rotational Speed: 868.22 km/h
  • Axis Tilt: 25.19°
  • Orbit Period: 687 Earth days
  • Day Length: 24 hours 37 minutes
  • Temperature: -140°C to 20°C

Fun Fact: Mars is home to Olympus Mons, the largest known volcano in the Solar System, standing at nearly three times the height of Mount Everest.

Jupiter

Jupiter, the largest planet in our Solar System, is a gas giant with a complex system of moons and a distinctive Great Red Spot. Its massive size and gravitational influence have earned it the nickname "King of the Planets".

  • Size: 139,820 km diameter
  • Age: 4.603 billion years
  • Rotational Speed: 45,300 km/h
  • Axis Tilt: 3.13°
  • Orbit Period: 11.86 Earth years
  • Day Length: 9 hours 56 minutes
  • Temperature: -145°C (cloud tops)

Fun Fact: Jupiter's Great Red Spot is a giant storm that has been raging for at least 400 years and is large enough to fit two or three Earths inside it.

Saturn

Saturn, famous for its stunning ring system, is a gas giant composed mainly of hydrogen and helium. Its low density means it could theoretically float in a giant bathtub of water.

  • Size: 116,460 km diameter
  • Age: 4.503 billion years
  • Rotational Speed: 36,840 km/h
  • Axis Tilt: 26.73°
  • Orbit Period: 29.46 Earth years
  • Day Length: 10 hours 42 minutes
  • Temperature: -178°C (cloud tops)

Fun Fact: Saturn's rings are made mostly of water ice, with some rocky debris and dust. They extend up to 175,000 miles from the planet but are typically only about 30 feet thick.

Uranus

Uranus, the third-largest planet in our Solar System, is an ice giant with a tilted axis of rotation that causes extreme seasons. Its blue-green color comes from methane in its atmosphere.

  • Size: 50,724 km diameter
  • Age: 4.503 billion years
  • Rotational Speed: 9,320 km/h
  • Axis Tilt: 97.77°
  • Orbit Period: 84 Earth years
  • Day Length: 17 hours 14 minutes
  • Temperature: -224°C (cloud tops)

Fun Fact: Uranus rotates on its side, likely due to a collision with an Earth-sized object billions of years ago. This gives it seasons that last for 20-plus years.

Neptune

Neptune, the windiest planet in our Solar System, is a distant ice giant with a dynamic atmosphere. It was the first planet located through mathematical predictions rather than direct observation.

  • Size: 49,244 km diameter
  • Age: 4.503 billion years
  • Rotational Speed: 9,660 km/h
  • Axis Tilt: 28.32°
  • Orbit Period: 164.79 Earth years
  • Day Length: 16 hours 6 minutes
  • Temperature: -218°C (cloud tops)

Fun Fact: Neptune's winds can reach speeds of 2,100 km/h, making it the windiest planet in our Solar System. These winds drive huge storms like the Great Dark Spot.
